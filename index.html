Our Projects

Where your support goes

Health · Ni-kshay Mitra

Nikshay Poshan Project

SAHAS registered as a Ni-Kshay Mitra partner in 2023. With funds raised from Trustees, we adopted 10 poor and needy TB patients for a year in March 2024, distributing WHO-approved dry nutritious food packets to them on World TB Day. This year, with the help of friends and well-wishers, we've reached about 22 patients. An appeal: sponsoring one patient for a year costs around just Rs. 1,000/- a month.

Sponsor a patient →
Education · Mobility

Cycle Project — Hope for education

In rural India, more than 60% of children drop out of school after 5th grade — high schools are far away, and 32.5% of girls drop out at the secondary level for want of a safe, affordable way to get there. SAHAS collects and refurbishes discarded bicycles, or funds new ones, so the journey to school becomes safer, faster, and life becomes a little easier. A bicycle costs roughly Rs. 4-5K new, around Rs. 2K second-hand — even Rs. 500/- from you moves us toward that goal.

Community pool

Child Sponsorship

Child sponsorship enables you to support children, their families and entire communities — giving them the chance to grow up healthy, educated and safe. Funds are pooled across five pillars: education, clean water & sanitation, food & nutrition, healthcare, and income training & empowerment. You can sponsor an infrastructure need such as a bicycle, or school essentials like uniforms, books and bags.

Education · Tuition

3E — Educate, Encourage, Elevate

We are in touch with schools to run a tuition assistance program for academically inclined, financially weak students, with yearly assistance based on academic performance through Standard 10th. To start, the founder members of SAHAS have each decided to sponsor one child, so that at least half a dozen are taken care of this academic year. A small amount of Rs. 8,000/- a year is enough to put a child through school — you select the child, and a report with photo is sent once the school year's need is funded.

Sponsor a child's year →
Public health

Health

Tuberculosis remains a serious health threat, and poor nutrition among the marginalised makes them especially vulnerable — more than 40 thousand are affected in Mumbai alone. Medical treatment is free, but nutrition, for the patient and their whole family, is not. As a self-financed organisation, SAHAS became a Ni-kshay Mitra and now supplies the WHO-recommended food basket to patients under the government's Nikshay Poshan Yojana, alongside our own funds.

Our Story

How SAHAS began

SAHAS was conceived by a few of us post-retirement, free from the shackles of a scheduled work life, wanting to give back to society a little of what our country gave us, inspired by an article about a few friends from a locality who had come together to provide aid to the needy which they, as individuals, were not able to.

Seven of us then — some retirees, some still working, one a housewife, one a doctor — decided we would collect a thousand rupees each, every month, so that at opportune moments we'd be able to give our small contribution where it was needed. As luck would have it, floods devastated Assam right around the time our coffers reached about fifty thousand rupees, and we emptied our collection.

Then Himanshu from Dharavi Art told us the children of the Dharavi slums could do with some help, and around the same time we read about Hatkalwadi's severe water shortage. That project was too large for seven of us to handle alone, but it gave us an idea: we needed to be organised so others could join us with their own generosity. After much discussion we agreed on a name — SAHAS, courage — and applied to the Charity Commission to register as an Association.

Even as the paperwork was underway, one of us had an accident and was laid up with a broken back. Then COVID happened, and everything stalled for two years; more of us retired, and our beloved Doctor Aunty passed away. By late 2022 we were back on track — a bank account for transparency, and our 80G tax exemption completed with friends' help. When COVID struck, Dr Padmini Desai and Dr Ajit Desai suggested we contribute toward medical equipment for treating patients, and Himanshu came back for the slum children's food. We could do both.

Individually, most of the founders already do their bit — volunteering with orphanages, working with temple trusts, sponsoring a girl child's education or their house-help's. Together, we've decided to concentrate first on education, sponsoring six kids' education ourselves, and on adopting TB patients to look after their treatment and their family's health. Everything requires money, and donors are our backbone.
